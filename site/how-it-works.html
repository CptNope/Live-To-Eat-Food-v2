How Live to Eat Food works

One person’s honest food finds around Worcester and Central Mass, turned into places you can find, save and actually go to. Here’s what the labels mean and how to reach us. The full rules are in the editorial and disclosure policy.

LTEF · How it works

Restaurants can’t buy Tina’s opinion.

Tina pays for her meals unless we say otherwise, right on the page. When a restaurant hosts her, the page says Hosted before her take. Ads are labeled ads. Being listed is free, forever. Paying never changes what she says.

LTEF · How it works · The promise

What the labels mean

Tina ate here · Oct 2026

She visited, paid, ate and wrote the take. The month is when she went.

Hosted

The restaurant invited Tina and covered the meal. Her take and verdicts are still her own, and a line above them says who paid. Hosted visits never get the stamp.

Paid partnership with [brand]

A brand paid Tina to make a post. It’s an ad in her voice: no verdict, never ranked, never on Roulette or in guides.

Verified business

The owner confirmed the hours and details. It isn’t an endorsement.

Tina hasn’t been yet

Listed so the map is complete. No take, no verdict, no handwriting.

Sponsored

A paid placement. Never bigger than the picks around it, never in Tina’s hand.

Referral link

We may earn a fee if you book. Never inside Tina’s take.

Enhanced listing

The owner pays for extra photos, a menu and booking links on their page. It never changes Tina’s take or where the place appears.

Charter merchant

One of the founding restaurants that got Enhanced free for 90 days at launch, in return for the Featured decal. It’s a business deal, not a pick.

Tina’s five verdicts

Regular rotationDon’t skip itWorth a tryYour callI’d pass

Instead of stars, Tina gives a place one of these five phrases, from Regular rotation (she keeps going back) to I’d pass, and only a place she paid for and ate at. Nobody else writes them, and nothing pays for one. Until she picks, a place page says “Verdict coming”.
