The brand book

Everything needed to build Live to Eat Food on screen and on cotton, specified down to the hex. If a rule here and a hunch disagree, the rule wins; if a rule and Tina disagree, Tina wins.

LTEF brand book · v1 proposal · October 2026 · Prepared for Tina Vo

Brand strategy

The friend who always knows where to eat.

That’s the position. Not a directory, not a review site, not a blog. A real person with ten years of Worcester meals behind every answer, and the receipts to prove she paid.

Every pick is a real meal.

  • Every recommendation traces to a meal Tina ate and paid for, unless the page says otherwise.
  • Paid placements are always labeled Sponsored, look different from picks, and never touch a rating, a verdict or her take.
  • Being listed is free. Paying never changes what Tina says.

Personality, in this order

Warm

We sound like Tina telling a friend where to go tonight: generous, specific, a little bossy about the good stuff. Warmth shows in daylight colours, soft corners and food that looks good enough to grab.

If warm and playful clash, warm wins.

Playful

Stickers, a spin-the-wheel, Woo-pons and chunky type. Play is how the brand invites you in, never how it dodges a straight answer.

Play never touches trust labels or ratings.

Trustworthy

Visible every time: the stamp, the visit month, the “Sponsored” hatch, the date hours were checked. Trust is third in tone and first in rules.

No joke is worth a blurred label.

Audience

The followers

Roughly 25K on Instagram and 16K on TikTok who already trust Tina’s taste and want her picks in one searchable place.

The “where tonight?” crowd

Worcester and Central Mass locals, students and visitors deciding on a phone, often for two or more people, often hungry right now.

Owners and partners

Restaurants who want to claim a listing, run a Woo-pon or sponsor a giveaway, and need to see that paid and earned are kept apart.

Rules

Proportion

  • A page is roughly 60% butter or vinyl, 25% one other pastel field (lemon or pistachio), 10% basil, 5% yolk, tomato and illustration colour.
  • One field colour owns a whole sheet. Don’t stripe pastels inside one section.
  • Peach appears on one sheet per page at most.
  • Yolk never fills an area wider than a button.

Meaning

  • Never use colour alone to say something. Sponsored is the word Sponsored plus the hatch plus oat; Pass/Fail always has an icon and a word.
  • Tomato means Tina wrote it. It never marks a sale, an error, an alert, a link or an ad.
  • Errors use basil text with an icon on a peach field, plus a plain sentence saying what to do.
  • Focus: 3px basil outline at 3px offset with a 6px white halo, visible on every field.

Rules

  • Sentence case for headings and buttons. All caps only on the stamp rim and the tiny printed tags on stickers and merch; never on screen UI.
  • Bagel Fat One only at 20px and up; below that it clogs. Never for paragraphs.
  • Tracking: Bagel −0.01em; everything else 0. Never tighter than −0.02em.
  • Headings use text-wrap: balance. Body never centred beyond three lines.
  • Prices and times use tabular numerals (font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums).

LTEF brand book · 3 · Typography

Logo & marks

One wordmark in three lockups, one trust stamp, one avatar, one deals ticket. All files are outlined SVG in assets/logo, so they render without the fonts.

How it’s drawn

  • Line 1: “Live to Eat” in Bagel Fat One, tracking −2/1000 em.
  • Line 2: “Food”, scaled until it’s exactly as wide as line 1 (about 1.6× the size). Gap between lines: 0.16× line-1 size.
  • The eggs: both o’s are fried eggs. White, slightly wobbly 9-point blob; basil ring 7.8% of the Food size; yolk circle #FFC53D, radius 10.4% of the Food size, offset up-right, with a 3.4% basil outline and one white highlight at −32°.
  • Colour: basil letters, white whites, yolk yolks. Reversed: white letters on basil, yolks stay yolk.
  • Sticker border: white die-cut following the letters at 16% of the line-1 size, then a 1.5px kiss-cut line.
  • Clear space: the cap height of “L” on every side. Minimum: 96px / 1 in wide stacked; 120px / 1.25 in for the one-line lockup.

The trust stamp

Tina ate here

The badge that says the pick is real. It’s the most important mark in the system, so its rules are strict.

  • Shape: 28-scallop bottle-cap edge, matcha fill, 4u basil outline; a dotted ring just inside; a white centre disc at 69% of the radius.
  • Rim: “EVERY PICK IS A REAL MEAL” across the top, “PAID FOR BY TINA” along the bottom, Bricolage Grotesque 760 caps.
  • Centre: “Tina / ate here” in Shantell Sans 720, tomato, rotated −6°, with a hand-drawn underline; a yolk dot at 12 o’clock.
  • Always paired with the visit month in live text beside it (“Tina ate here · Sep 2026”).
  • Only on places Tina visited and paid for. Never on sponsored content, never on a place she hasn’t been, never as decoration.
  • Minimum: 64px / 0.75 in. Below that, use the text label Tina ate here.

LTEF brand book · 4 · Logo & marks

Motifs & illustration

The recurring language is the food sticker: one dish, flat colour, a basil outline and a white die-cut border, stuck onto a pastel sheet at a slight angle.

Drawing rules

  • Draw on a 120-unit square. Outline 4u basil, round joins and caps. White die-cut 9u around the silhouette.
  • Two or three flat fills from Lemon, Yolk, Pistachio, Matcha, Peach, Butter, plus illustration-only Chili and Latte. No gradients, no textures, no shading.
  • At most one white highlight (an ellipse at about −30°).
  • One dish per sticker, recognisable at 48px.
  • No faces on food, no hearts in eyes, no drool. Food is the star; it doesn’t need to wink.
  • Detail lines inside (pleats, noodles, ice) use 3–3.5u.

The starter set

Grow the set from Tina’s actual posts: when she covers a dish often, it earns a sticker.

Placing stickers

  • Stickers live on fields, around content, never over it. Never on top of a food photo, a price, a label or body text.
  • Up to 10 per desktop hero, 3 per phone screen, 0–2 inside content sheets.
  • Vary the tilt (−8° to +8°); never two neighbours at the same angle. The wordmark tilts 3° at most.
  • Print the kiss-cut: on screens, a 1.5px basil line at 16% opacity, 5px outside the white border.
  • Sponsored modules get no stickers at all.

Motion

  • One authored move: the peel. On hover a sticker lifts 4px, turns 3° more and its shadow deepens (500ms, ease-out cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)).
  • The roulette wheel spins about 4–5 turns over 3.2s and lands; nothing else spins.
  • No scroll-triggered entrances, nothing hidden at rest. prefers-reduced-motion removes all of it.

LTEF brand book · 5 · Motifs & illustration

Voice & tone

Write like Tina talking to a friend who just asked where to eat: direct, specific, warm, a little bossy about the good stuff.

Reviews

  • Specific beats enthusiastic. “Order the crispy rice” beats “amazing!”
  • Say where: street address and neighborhood, every time.
  • First person is Tina’s. Takes and notes say “I”; the interface says “you” and “Tina”.
  • Her words are real. Quotes come from her posts and link to them. Never write a line for her.
  • Verdicts replace stars: I’d get this again · Don’t skip this · Bring friends · Worth the drive · Good, not the one

Sponsored

  • The word Sponsored appears on every paid thing, in text, before the reader sees the offer.
  • Sponsored copy is written in the brand’s voice, never Tina’s first person, never in her hand.
  • Prize donors and paid partners are named: “Prize donated by [restaurant].”
  • Paid placements never sit in a ranked list, never in a top slot, never larger than the picks around them.
  • Referral links say so: “We may earn a fee if you book.”

Community

  • Credit first: every reader photo shows the handle, and only with permission.
  • Celebrate the order, not the person’s looks or body.
  • Disagreement is welcome; dunking on restaurants or staff isn’t.
  • Giveaway copy says how to enter, when it ends and where the rules are, in that order.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do: “Nothing open that late in Holden. Try Worcester, or switch to tomorrow.”

LTEF brand book · 6 · Voice & tone

Imagery

Food first, real over styled, no stock heroes. Photos are Tina’s own (or the restaurant’s, with permission), framed like a sticker: a white border and soft corners.

The frames above are shot briefs. Stickers stand in until Tina’s photos are dropped in; never replace them with stock.

Do

  • Daylight or the room’s own light. Window seats at lunch are the brand’s natural lighting.
  • Show texture: the crackle, the drip, the steam.
  • Keep colour honest. No filters or grading toward the palette; the pastels are around the photo, not in it.
  • Frame photos with a 10px white border, 20px radius (14px inside).
  • Alt text names the dish and the place: “Pork and chive dumplings at [restaurant], Shrewsbury St.”

Don’t

  • Stock photos of food, people or restaurants, ever.
  • Generated images of Tina or of dishes presented as real.
  • Stickers, text or logos over the food. The stamp may overlap a photo’s corner by up to a quarter of its width, never the dish.
  • Building facades or street scenes as heroes (the old direction).
  • Heavy vignettes, tilt-shift, or duotones.
